## 1. Abstract

- One-paragraph summary: hammock (refactored Python + C++ minimizer-HLL sketcher) is sensitive enough to reproduce the known cross-reference-genome robustness of H3K27ac peak landscapes within a species. Applied to cross-species H3K27ac peak FASTAs, however, sequence-level sketching consistently recovers a species signal rather than the tissue signal reported in the alignment-based literature — even after orthologous-peak filtering. We characterize the gap and motivate coordinate-space liftover as the necessary preprocessing for cross-species comparison.
- Headline numbers: effect size ~0.10 Jaccard for cross-reference within-tissue vs different-tissue (Wilcoxon p ≈ 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁰); for cross-species (12 samples, 6 tissues × 2 species), 1/80 sweep cells reaches tissue dominance and only at near-zero ARI — within noise.

## 2. Introduction

- Motivation: peak-FASTA similarity sketches as a fast, lightweight alternative to alignment-based or peak-overlap-based comparison of ChIP-seq data.
- Prior work on tissue-over-species clustering in epigenomics: [@Yue2014], [@Lin2014], [@Roadmap2015], [@ENCODE2020], [@BarbosaMorais2012], [@Merkin2012].
- Knowledge gap: existing tissue-over-species results rely on aligned peak intensities at orthologous coordinates; whether *sequence-level* sketching of peak FASTAs can recover the same signal is an open question.
- Contribution: a refactored hammock implementation, plus a two-experiment validation framework (cross-reference robustness + tissue-over-species clustering) that characterizes what minimizer-HLL sketching of peak FASTAs can and cannot do.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1. hammock implementation

- Python orchestrator + C++ extension (pybind11) with HLL register backing, xxh64 ingestion, Ertl 2017 cardinality estimator, register-equality Jaccard. Parity with the original `hammock` (Bonnie & Langmead) is byte-equal on Modes A/B/C/D where directly comparable.
- Standalone `hammock-cpp` binary for max-speed Mode B (no Python in the loop).
- Mode D (FASTA): minimizer-based; configurable k-mer and window size; HLL precision controls memory / accuracy trade-off.
- Configuration: pip-installed into a conda env (`claude-ref-comparison`, Python 3.10, bioconda `digest`) on a SLURM cluster; jobs orchestrated via Snakemake with the SLURM executor.

### 3.2. Data

- **Experiment A — Cross-reference robustness:** 3 human H3K27ac ChIP-seq samples (heart `ENCSR175ABH`, liver `ENCSR864OOO`, lung `ENCSR954JMZ`) aligned to GRCh37, GRCh38, and CHM13 — 9 sample × ref combinations. Peaks called via nf-core/chipseq + MACS3 (broad and narrow modes).
- **Experiment B — Tissue-over-species clustering:** 6 matched tissues across human (GRCh38) and mouse (mm10): heart, liver, lung, spleen, small intestine, testis. **12 samples** total.
  - Human (GRCh38): `ENCSR175ABH` heart, `ENCSR864OOO` liver, `ENCSR954JMZ` lung, `ENCSR436JNB` spleen, `ENCSR655XLM` small intestine, `ENCSR136ZQZ` testis.
  - Mouse (mm10, all Bing Ren / UCSD Mouse ENCODE LICR, 2-month B6NCrl): `ENCSR000CDF` heart, `ENCSR000CDH` liver, `ENCSR884MYD` lung, `ENCSR000CDJ` spleen, `ENCSR000CCQ` small intestine, `ENCSR000CCU` testis.
  - One protocol caveat to flag in methods: human small intestine (`ENCSR655XLM`) is UCSD/Ren lab, 50 bp SE, 1 replicate — all other human samples are Bernstein/Broad, 76 bp SE, 2 replicates. See `docs/methods_notes.md`.
- See `config/exp_a_metadata.tsv` and `config/exp_b_metadata.tsv` for sample manifests.

### 3.3. Pipeline

- nf-core/chipseq for FASTQ → BAM → peak BED (one nf-core run per (reference, peak_type) — 8 runs total).
- bedtools getfasta to extract peak sequences from the source genome.
- hammock all-vs-all on peak FASTA lists, in Mode D, at a (k, w) parameter sweep.
- For redesign B2 (Section 4.3): orthologous-peak filter via `bedtools intersect` against UCSC phastCons elements (100-way for hg38, 60-way for mm10).
- For redesign B1 (Section 4.4): UCSC `liftOver` of mouse peaks `mm10 → GRCh38` via `mm10ToHg38.over.chain`, then bedtools getfasta from GRCh38 — both species' inputs now live in a single coordinate space.
- Statistical evaluation in R (Wilcoxon for exp_a; hierarchical clustering + Adjusted Rand Index for exp_b).
- Parameter sweep: `k ∈ {5, 8, 10, 15, 20}`, `w ∈ {5, 8, 10, 15, 20, 30}`, filtered to `w ≥ k`. 20 (k, w) cells total; `(5, 5)` is the slowest cell (12 h runtime) and is included.
- Cross-cell ARI aggregation script: `scripts/aggregate_ari.py`.

## 4. Results

### 4.1. Cross-reference robustness (Experiment A) — hammock recovers expected signal

- Headline result: for every (k, w) cell in the sweep, same-tissue cross-reference similarity is significantly higher than different-tissue similarity (Wilcoxon `p < 10⁻⁵` everywhere; the smallest p-value ~10⁻¹⁰; ARI-equivalent effect-size 0.05–0.11).
- Best operating point: `(k=10, w=20)` for broad peaks, effect size **0.107** (median 0.106 within-tissue cross-ref vs median 0.001 different-tissue).
- Visual demonstration:
  - **Fig 2a:** `results/exp_a/broad/sweep_effect_size.png` — (k × w) heatmap of effect size for broad peaks; clearly identifies `k=10` as the optimal column.
  - **Fig 2b:** `results/exp_a/narrow/sweep_effect_size.png` — same for narrow peaks; same optimum.
  - **Fig 3:** `results/exp_a/broad/k10_w20/cross_ref_validation.png` — 2-panel figure for the best (k, w): comparison-type boxplot (left) + 9×9 pairwise-similarity heatmap (right) showing three uniformly-bright diagonal tissue blocks.
- Supporting table:
  - **Table 1:** `results/exp_a/broad/k10_w20/cross_ref_stats.tsv` — Wilcoxon results (n, medians, p-value) at the chosen operating point.

### 4.2. Tissue-over-species clustering on raw peak FASTAs (Experiment B, baseline) — species dominates

- Headline result: across all 40 sweep cells (20 (k, w) × broad+narrow), **1 of 40 cells** is tissue-dominant — but only marginally (`exp_b/broad/k=5, w=8`: ARI tissue **0.057** vs ARI species **0.046**, both near zero). The other 39 cells are clearly species-dominant; most saturate at species ARI **0.555** vs tissue ARI **−0.152**.
- Mechanism: raw peak FASTAs contain species-specific sequence (peaks in different species have negligible 15-mer overlap even at orthologous loci), so the sketch sees genome composition, not tissue identity.
- Visual demonstration:
  - **Fig 4a:** `results/exp_b/broad/k15_w15/dendrogram.png` — typical species-dominant tree (all 6 humans tight, mouse split into 5 small clusters).
  - **Fig 4b (supplementary):** `results/exp_b/broad/k5_w8/dendrogram.png` — the marginal "winner"; visibly closer to noise than to a real tissue split.

### 4.3. Orthologous-peak filtering does **not** rescue tissue signal (Experiment B redesign B2)

- Method: filter both species' peaks to phastCons-conserved blocks (UCSC phastConsElements100way / 60way) before extracting FASTA. Both species' inputs are now restricted to evolutionarily-conserved regions, compressing the species-specific component of the sketch.
- Result with 12 samples: **0 of 40 sweep cells** are tissue-dominant under B2 filtering. Even the cells that came closest to parity in the raw experiment revert to species dominance once the orthology filter is applied.
- Reproducibility note: an earlier 6-sample run found 1/38 cells tissue-dominant under B2 at (broad, k=5, w=8) with ARI tissue 0.444 vs species −0.176 (cross-species liver pair). That result did not survive doubling the sample size — strong evidence it was an underpowered fluke driven by the small sample's degree-of-freedom budget rather than a real biological signal. We retain the prior result in supplementary material for transparency.
- Interpretation: phastCons-based filtering reduces species-specific *sequence content* in each peak FASTA, but does not align orthologous peaks to each other — a 15-mer of conserved sequence in mouse liver and a different 15-mer of conserved sequence in human liver still don't match. Conservation filtering is insufficient; coordinate-space alignment is needed.
- Visual demonstration:
  - **Fig 5:** `results/exp_b_conserved/broad/k5_w8/dendrogram.png` — the former "best B2" cell; with 12 samples this is species-dominant.

### 4.4. Coordinate-space liftover (Experiment B redesign B1) — placeholder

- Method (planned): UCSC `liftOver` of every mouse peak from mm10 to GRCh38 via `mm10ToHg38.over.chain`. Unmappable peaks (~30% based on published lift-rate estimates) are dropped. Re-extract FASTA from GRCh38 at the lifted intervals; both species' inputs are now expressed in the same coordinate system. Re-run the (k, w) sweep + clustering.
- Expected: with species composition entirely factored out at the *coordinate* level, any remaining sketch similarity reflects orthologous-peak landscape. If tissue-over-species clustering exists in this data at all, B1 is the experiment that should expose it.
- Negative-result possibility: if B1 also fails to recover tissue signal at 12 samples, the conclusion is stronger — k-mer sketches of peak FASTAs cannot recover cross-species tissue identity even when coordinates are aligned, and the literature claim depends on intensity (peak height / coverage) information, not sequence content.
- Status: rules added to `workflow/Snakefile`; outputs land at `results/exp_b_lifted/{broad,narrow}/k*_w*/`. Results to be reported here once the run completes.

### 4.5. Parameter sweep and operating-point selection

- Frame both experiments' (k, w) sweeps as a *characterization tool* — a map of which biological regimes are accessible at which sketch parameters, not parameter tuning for a single answer.
- Exp A: any k ≥ 8 works; optimum at k=10 with w ≥ 10.
- Exp B (raw, conserved): no (k, w) cell works at 12 samples; species composition swamps tissue regardless of conservation filtering.
- Exp B (lifted, pending): TBD.
- Visual demonstration:
  - **Fig 6:** side-by-side replot of Exp A vs Exp B (raw/conserved/lifted) (k × w) effect-size heatmaps — same axes, different colormap signal — emphasizing that within-species the parameter space is broad and within cross-species (without coordinate-space alignment) the parameter space is empty.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1. What hammock can and cannot do

- Within-species, within-tissue: hammock cleanly distinguishes biological identity from technical (reference-genome) variation across GRCh37/GRCh38/CHM13.
- Cross-species without coordinate alignment: hammock fails to recover tissue signal under both raw and orthology-filtered preprocessing, even at 12 samples spread across 6 tissues. This is a substantive limitation of any exact-match k-mer sketch applied to peak FASTAs from different reference genomes; it is not specific to hammock's implementation.
- Cross-species *with* coordinate-space liftover: under evaluation (Section 4.4).

### 5.2. Comparison to prior work

- The literature claim that "tissue beats species" in epigenomic data ([@Yue2014], [@Lin2014], [@Roadmap2015]) was established with methods that operate in shared coordinate space (peak-overlap matrices, intensity-vector clustering at orthologous loci). The present sequence-level sketching analysis suggests that the tissue signal in the literature lives in *which coordinates are peaks*, not in *what the peak sequences look like*.
- An earlier 6-sample iteration of this study reported a 1/38 hit for tissue-dominant clustering under conservation filtering at (broad, k=5, w=8). That result did not survive doubling the sample size to 12 — a cautionary tale about hit-rates at small N in cross-species sweeps.

### 5.3. Limitations of the current study

- 6 tissues × 2 species × 1 replicate (12 samples for Exp B) — moderate statistical power; some tissue pairs are still represented by a single ENCSR per species, and (sm. int. human, `ENCSR655XLM`) is a protocol outlier (different ENCODE lab from the other 5 human samples).
- Conservation filtering uses one specific track per genome (phastCons 100-way / 60-way). Alternative orthology evidence (cCRE conservation column, VISTA enhancer set, syntenic blocks) is not evaluated.
- Liftover-based redesign (B1) is in progress (Section 4.4); the cross-species conclusion is preliminary until that experiment completes.

### 5.4. Future work

- B1 (liftover) evaluation — pending (rules wired into Snakefile; see Section 4.4).
- If B1 also fails: explicit test of the *intensity* hypothesis by sketching peak intervals weighted by signal coverage rather than treating each peak FASTA as a flat sequence set.
- Replicate expansion: 2–3 replicates per tissue per species to sharpen ARI estimates and stabilize the small-cell behaviour at the (k=5, w=8) corner.
- Alternative conservation tracks (VISTA enhancer set, ENCODE cCRE conservation column).
- Application to within-species multi-tissue clustering at scale (e.g., the full ENCODE H3K27ac reference epigenome set) — where the orthology question is moot and the cross-reference robustness result (Section 4.1) suggests hammock is ready to deploy.

## 6. Conclusion

- hammock is sensitive enough to recover the established cross-reference-robustness property of H3K27ac peak landscapes (Section 4.1).
- Sequence-level sketching of peak FASTAs is insufficient on its own to recover the literature's cross-species tissue signal (Section 4.2), and orthology-aware preprocessing via phastCons filtering does not close the gap (Section 4.3).
- The work characterizes the gap between sequence-level and coordinate-space analysis for cross-species epigenomic comparison and motivates coordinate-space liftover as the necessary preprocessing for any sequence-based cross-species peak comparison (Section 4.4).
